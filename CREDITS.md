# Contribution credit

## Our policy

We give credit by name to all contributors. When submitting a contribution, we
expect contributors to include accurate information about who contributed to the
work.

Credit is not limited to code or commit authorship. Documentation, examples,
testing, bug reports, debugging, design, review, and project maintenance also
count. [AUTHORS.md](AUTHORS.md) is our public contributor list;
[CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) describes what work we accept and how review
works. Attribution does not imply that a proposed change will be accepted.

## Recording credit

- Add your name to `AUTHORS.md` in your contribution if it is not already there.
  Use one bullet per person and append new names without duplicating existing
  entries. Use the name you want published, including Unicode or a pseudonym;
  a GitHub profile link is optional. A legal name or email address is not required.
- In the PR description, identify the people involved and their contributions.
  Include collaborators whose work does not appear in the commit author field,
  such as the person who reported or diagnosed the bug or supplied a test case.
  Confirm each collaborator's preferred public name before adding their entry.
- Preserve existing attribution when adapting, editing, or rewriting someone
  else's work. Link the original issue, PR, or other source, and name both the
  original contributor and the people who revised it. Do not present another
  person's work as your own.
- For jointly authored commits, use Git's `Co-authored-by` trailers when the
  collaborators agree to publish the required identity information. Trailers do
  not replace the `AUTHORS.md` entry or the PR's explanation of contributions.
- If your contribution is a report, review, or other work without a PR, ask in
  the relevant issue or discussion to have your name added, or submit a small
  `AUTHORS.md` update yourself.

When similar contributions overlap, maintainers choose what to integrate on its
merits, not just submission order. Credit everyone whose work, diagnosis, or
ideas informed the accepted change, with links to the relevant contributions.
The contributor list does not rank people by commit count, change size, or role.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Before merging, check that the PR identifies its contributors and that their
preferred names appear in `AUTHORS.md`. Preserve attribution in the squash-merge
message, including agreed co-author trailers and links to original work. If a
maintainer finishes or rewrites a contribution, credit both the original work
and the subsequent changes; integration edits must not erase the original
contributor's credit.

If a report or proposal is declined but later informs accepted work, acknowledge
that contribution when it is used. Contributors may also decline public naming.

## Corrections and name changes

Request additions, corrections, or a preferred-name change at any time through a
pull request or the [issue tracker](https://github.com/peter-cudmore/coker/issues).
For a sensitive request, contact the maintainer at
[drpetercudmore@gmail.com](mailto:drpetercudmore@gmail.com) instead of posting personal
information publicly. Maintainers will correct the current contributor list and
relevant attribution records. Changes are recorded in public Git history;
updating a name does not remove earlier versions from that history.
